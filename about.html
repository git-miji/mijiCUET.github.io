About

From optical networks to the nanoscale

Eight years engineering light through fibre, two master's degrees in computing and electrical engineering, and doctoral research that brings both together at the nanoscale.

I am a doctoral researcher at the South Dakota School of Mines & Technology, pursuing a Ph.D. in Nano Science and Nanoengineering with a focus on nanophotonics and plasmonics. I hold an M.S. in Computer Science & Engineering from the same institution (2024) and a B.Sc. in Electrical & Electronic Engineering from Chittagong University of Engineering & Technology (2013), and have also pursued graduate study in Electrical Engineering at South Dakota Mines.

My route into nanophotonics ran through industry rather than straight from a lecture hall. After my bachelor's I spent eight years in telecommunications and IT — designing optical fibre backbones, planning PON and FTTH deployments, running soft-switch and internet services, and later working in cybersecurity. Years of treating light as an engineering medium, with real budgets and real failure modes, gave me an instinct for optical systems that I now bring to the nanoscale.

Graduate study sharpened the computational half. I worked on machine learning, data-driven system identification of dynamical systems, and the security of cyber-physical systems — including a comparative study of Kolmogorov–Arnold networks against conventional neural networks for discovering system dynamics.

Those two threads meet in computational nanophotonics, and that intersection is the subject of my doctoral work. Modelling plasmonic structures well demands both physical intuition about how fields behave and the numerical discipline to know when a simulation is telling the truth — rigorous full-wave modelling, accelerated and eventually inverted by machine learning.

Outside research I have spent much of my time building organisations: founding general secretary of two associations, a year in the President's Leadership Academy, and continuing work supporting international and graduate students.
